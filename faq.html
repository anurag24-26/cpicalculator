FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Short answers about CPI, CGPA, SGPA, percentage conversion and attendance. For worked examples, see the guides.

What does CPI stand for?

CPI stands for Cumulative Performance Index. It is the credit-weighted average of your grade points across all completed courses, usually on a 10-point scale. See What is CPI? for details.

How is CPI calculated?

Multiply each subject's credits by its grade points, add the results, and divide by the total credits: CPI = Σ(Credits × Grade point) ÷ Σ Credits. The CPI calculator does this for you.

Is CPI the same as CGPA?

The idea is the same: a credit-weighted average over your programme. The name depends on the institution, and the rules for backlogs, repeated or non-credit courses can differ, so follow your university's regulations.

What is the difference between SGPA and CGPA?

SGPA covers a single semester, while CGPA covers all semesters completed so far. CGPA is the credit-weighted combination of your SGPAs.

How do I calculate SGPA?

Multiply the credits of each subject in the semester by its grade points, add them up, and divide by the semester's total credits. A worked example is in the SGPA guide.

Can I find my CGPA by averaging my SGPAs?

Only if every semester has the same number of credits. Otherwise, weight each SGPA by its credits, as the SGPA to CGPA converter does. For example, SGPA 8.0 with 20 credits and 9.0 with 25 credits gives 8.56, not the plain average of 8.50.

How do I convert CGPA to percentage?

It depends on your university. Common rules include CGPA × 9.5, or (CGPA × 10) − 7.5. Pick your formula in the CGPA to percentage converter, or enter a custom multiplier.

Is CGPA × 9.5 the right formula for my university?

It is widely used and is the option provided here for GLA University, but it is not universal. Some universities use CGPA × 10, others subtract a fixed amount. Confirm the rule in your university's official notification before using the result on any application.

What attendance percentage is required?

Many universities require 75%, but some use 70% or 80%, and the rules for exam eligibility vary. Check your department's handbook, and use the attendance calculator with the right target.

How many classes do I need to attend to reach 75% attendance?

If you attend every upcoming class, you need ⌈(75 × Conducted − 100 × Attended) ÷ 25⌉ more classes. For 28 attended out of 40, that is 8 classes. See the attendance formula guide.

How many classes can I miss and still keep 75%?

The maximum is ⌊(100 × Attended ÷ 75) − Conducted⌋. With 36 attended out of 40 you can miss 8 more classes and stay at 75%, as long as you attend all the others.

Does medical or duty leave count towards attendance?

That is decided by your institution, not by a calculator. Many colleges allow condonation with valid documents, so ask your department what applies to you.

What CGPA is considered good?

It depends on your university and goals. Many students and employers treat 8.0 and above as strong, and roughly 6.0 to 7.0 as a common minimum for eligibility, but the right benchmark is the one set by the programme or company you are aiming for.

What CGPA do I need for placements?

It varies by company and year. Many mass recruiters ask for around 60% or 6.0 CGPA with no active backlogs, while some product and core companies expect 7.0 to 8.0 or more. Always read each company's official eligibility notice. More in the placement guide.

How can I raise my CGPA?

Focus on high-credit subjects, secure your internal marks, revise consistently and get help early. The effect of one semester depends on how many credits you have already completed. Tips are in the improvement guide.

What SGPA do I need to reach my target CGPA?

Use Required SGPA = (Target × (Done + Left) − Current × Done) ÷ Left. The required CGPA predictor calculates it and shows the highest CGPA still possible if the target is out of reach.

How do backlogs affect CPI or CGPA?

Policies differ. Often a failed course counts with zero grade points until you clear it, after which the new grade replaces or is capped. Check your university's rules, since the calculators here assume the grades you enter are the ones that count.

How does the grade calculator work?

Each component's score is converted to a fraction, multiplied by its weight, and the results are added and scaled to 100. You can leave a score blank for assessments that have not happened yet. Grade bands are an estimate and vary by university.

Are the results official?

No. These tools give quick estimates based on the standard formulas and the numbers you enter. Your official marksheet and university regulations always take priority.

Is my data stored or sent anywhere?

No. All calculations run in your browser, and the marks, credits and attendance you type are not uploaded or saved by this site.
